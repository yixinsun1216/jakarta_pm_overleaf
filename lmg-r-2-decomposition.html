LMG \(R^2\) Decomposition

To complement the mean contribution decomposition in the main text, we decompose the \(R^2\) of Equation 1 across predictors using the Lindeman-Merenda-Gold (LMG) method (Lindeman, Merenda, and Gold 1980). In multiple regressions, the contribution of a predictor to explained variance depends on which other predictors are already in the model, rendering simple sequential decompositions sensitive to arbitrary ordering choices. LMG resolves the attribution problem that arises under predictor correlation by averaging each predictor’s marginal \(R^2\) contribution over all possible orderings in which predictors could enter the model. This method thus provides a consistent decomposition of explained variance. We implement this using the relaimpo package in R (Grömping 2006). The ranking of sources by \(R^2\) contribution is consistent with the mean contribution decomposition in the main text: outdoor ambient pollution accounts for the largest share, followed by household smoking and waste burning, with kitchen source, cooking hour, and distance to main road contributing minimally. For pollution spikes, smoking is by far the dominant contributor, while waste burning also contributes substantially.
